ABOUT PRODUCTS WEB MOTION

In 2020, amid widespread hygiene product shortages, a friend and I wondered — in our hyper-designed world, where every product competes for attention, why had bar soap been left behind? While most everyday objects had been reimagined as statement pieces, soap remained surprisingly stagnant — creative in scent and color, but stuck in its traditional shape.

A couple of years later, as I began teaching myself design software (Adobe Suite, Blender) more seriously, that question came back. I wanted to build something tangible that tied together everything I was learning: product design, branding, 3D modeling, motion design, web development. This was the genesis of Slug.

Over the course of this project, I've designed and produced six distinct soap bars (see products tab), each with different functional considerations. Beyond the physical products, I also developed a cohesive visual system that bridges digital and craft aesthetics. I've sold over 500 bars through select San Francisco retailers and direct-to-consumer channels.



















IDENTITY

Slug's visual identity went through many iterations as I built the brand. I've always been drawn to products with contemporary design, a sense of quality, and a shock factor. From the start, I knew Slug was more of a portfolio project than a mass-market soap brand, so I focused on making designs that felt authentic to my own tastes rather than adapting to broad consumer preferences.

When I think of soap, two brands come to mind: Dove , with its generic white box, and Dawn , which is vibrant blue with a duck on the bottle but still feels mass-produced. I wanted Slug to be the anthesis; artful, energetic, and bold. Most of the Slug's graphic design uses a high-contrast color palette, with dual-tone variations of purple, blue, green, and red.

Slug Soap primarily uses Monument Grotesk Mono and Eucrosia UPC Italic (with tight kerning) to create contrast in brand communications. Monument, a monospaced sans-serif, handles informational text like product descriptions and website content, prioritizing readability with its uniform spacing and clear letterforms. Eucrosia UPC Italic is louder and adds character. I used it for headers and emphasis to create visual interest against Monument's more structured monospace style.

There are two Slug logos. Originally, the brand was centered around the pixelated snail mark , but overtime I took the spiral out of that logo and stretched it because I liked how this design was simpler. This logo reflects the brand's digital-meets-handmade aesthetic.



















PACKAGING

For the Star, Grip, Flower, and Shell soaps, I designed packaging that captures the brand's personality while enhancing the overall product experience. My goal was to create a versatile container accommodating most soap shapes in a cohesive, memorable way.

Each soap comes in a minimal off-white box (shown at right) featuring an embossed snail logo on the front and 'slug' on the bottom, creating a tactile experience that mirrors the textural quality of the soap itself and engages users before they even see the product.

Opening the box reveals an energetic interior featuring Slug Soap's full cast of characters rendered in two-tone dark and light green. This stark contrast between the understated exterior and dynamic interior creates an intentional reveal during unboxing.

The packaging uses 18pt 100% post-consumer waste paperboard, chosen for its embossing capability and environmental sustainability. The natural off-white tone of the recycled stock adds subtle depth to the exterior embossing, while the interior features custom green soy-based inks in two tones for the illustrated elements. The box uses a tabbed lock-bottom construction for stability and clean edges, ensuring structural integrity and a premium unboxing experience.

The Slug 2024 collection includes six designs, each rethinking traditional bar soap. Using glycerin-based materials let me explore transparency, color, and sculptural form while still making sure the soaps were effective and long-lasting.


































Mystery Soap was the first bar I designed. Inspired by insect lollipops and resin paperweights, these transparent bars contain objects suspended within them, transforming ordinary items into art.. The design also incorporates raised typography featuring 'slug' in block letters — an element meant to emphasize soap's inherently tactile nature. As the soap wears down, the way the surfaces and letters change creates new grip zones over time.


Cheese Soap emerged from a study in functional form. Its distinctive wedge shape offers a bold visual departure from traditional soap bars while incorporating practical design elements: the natural holes and indentations found in cheese provide intuitive grip points, addressing a key oversight in conventional oval soap design.


The Shell Soap explores how organic forms can reshape the traditional soap bar. Taking inspiration from the logarithmic spiral of a snail shell, it coils around a central axis, creating natural grip points and varying surface areas that make the bar both functional and visually dynamic. Each color variation has a complementary scent.

Flower Soap translates traditional floral fragrances into physical form. The six-petaled design provides multiple grip points while its structure maintains shape integrity throughout use. Each color comes with matching flower scent:
Green — Ylang-ylang
Blue — Chamomile
Pink — Cherry Blossom
Purple — Lavender

Star Soap is glow-in-the-dark and star-shaped. The star has five distinct points for gripping while its center maintains enough surface area for practical use.

The Grip Soap's geometric shape features angled ridges that provide optimal grip while the broad surfaces maintain practical functionality. The front face showcases an embossed Slug Soap logo, adding both branding and texture.


Merch Beyond soap, I created a screen-printed t-shirt, a risograph print, nalgene water bottles, and stickers.


Production Process I designed custom molds for all of the soaps in Blender and AutoCAD. Then I 3D printed and casted each design in silicone to create the mold. Each of the soaps have their own recipe based on their color and fragrance. Every bar takes about a month to cure.


I designed and developed 4 websites for Slug in 2024. The initial slugsoap.net launch featured a split-screen layout (v1) with a scrollable product feed on the left and fixed brand elements anchored on the right. While this architecture provided consistent navigation access, it failed to deliver the engaging user experience the brand required.

I redesigned the entire online shop with a grid-based layout that displays all products adjacently, creating a more immersive browsing experience. Instead of static product photography, I used animated .gifs that demonstrate product movement and texture. This dynamic visual approach significantly enhances user engagement while maintaining brand consistency and creating a more contemporary shopping interface that differentiates from traditional e-commerce websites.

The platform extends beyond the main store through two additional sites. The Slug archive functions as an experimental product database where each soap bar triggers audio feedback when selected, adding an interactive layer to the browsing experience. This creates a more engaging exploration of the product catalog beyond standard visual presentation.

Slug radio was developed as a microsite supporting the Slug t-shirt release. The site combines curated music playlists with rotating early-internet .gifs sourced from GeoCities.org, creating a nostalgic digital experience that reinforces the brand's unique aesthetic approach.
